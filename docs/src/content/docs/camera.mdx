## The repeated world and the antimeridian

MapLibre renders the world repeating horizontally: scrolling east past 180°
continues into another copy of the world at -180°. The library keeps the two
coordinate conventions apart with two types.

Values **read from the map** — <Api of="Viewport.visibleBounds" />,
<Api of="VisibleRegion" />, <Api of="MapState.positionFromScreenLocation" />,
and the position delivered to click handlers — use continuous longitudes that
preserve the world copy you see. They may extend past ±180°, and the visible
bounds can span more than 360° when more than one world fits in the viewport.
Within <Api of="VisibleBounds" />, east is always numerically greater than or
equal to west; it never uses the GeoJSON antimeridian encoding.

`VisibleBounds.wrapped()` converts to a GeoJSON `BoundingBox` with longitudes
wrapped into ±180°. When the wrapped bounds cross the antimeridian, the result
follows RFC 7946: the east longitude is less than the west longitude:

<Code code={region(camera, "repeated-world")} lang="kotlin" title="App.kt" />

Values **passed to the map** treat longitudes equivalent modulo 360° as the
same place. A camera target at longitude 190° looks at the same map as -170°.
Bounds fitting and camera constraints accept an antimeridian-crossing region in
either encoding: east less than west (GeoJSON), or east greater than 180°
(continuous). Fitting always frames the region the short way around.
<Api of="MapState.screenLocationFromPosition" /> projects the world copy
nearest the camera.

Features returned by <Api of="MapState.queryRenderedFeatures" /> keep the
coordinates the engine rendered, without normalization: a geometry that crosses
the antimeridian may come back split into pieces, and longitudes may fall
outside ±180° in either direction.
